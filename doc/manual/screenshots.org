* Screenshots

Mahogany takes screenshots with =grim= and saves them as PNG files in
=*screenshot-directory*= (=~/Pictures/Screenshots/= by default), named
after the time they were taken. Each is copied to the clipboard as
well (=*screenshot-copy-command*=, =wl-copy= by default).

- =screenshot-area= (=Print=): a translucent overlay covers the
  screen; drag with the pointer to choose the area. =Escape=, a click
  without a drag or another button cancels.
- =screenshot-window= (=Shift-Print=): the focused window, without its
  border.
- =screenshot-screen= (=Control-Print=): the whole current output.
- =screenshot-menu= lists them, each with and without editing.

** Editing

After a screenshot a message says where it went; with =satty=
installed, a click on the message opens the screenshot in it, to
crop, draw arrows, blur, and save or copy again. The message stays
=*screenshot-message-delay*= milliseconds (8 seconds). satty floats
over the tiled windows, by the default =*floating-rules*= (see
[[file:floating.org][Floating Windows]]).

=*screenshot-editor*= is the command, with =:file= in place of the
file; =*screenshot-edit*= says what the commands do about it:
=:click= (the message), =t= (open it right away, as before), =nil=
(never), or =:auto=, the default, which is =:click= when the program
is installed. The menu offers editing either way.

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
  ;; Edit in swappy instead of satty:
  (setf *screenshot-editor* '("swappy" "-f" :file))
  ;; Open the editor right after every screenshot:
  (setf *screenshot-edit* t)
  ;; Never edit, just save and copy:
  (setf *screenshot-edit* nil)
#+END_SRC

** Other keys

The bindings are in =*top-map*=; rebind them as any command:

#+BEGIN_SRC lisp
  (define-key *top-map* (kbd "s-S-s") #'screenshot-area)
#+END_SRC

** From Lisp

=(take-screenshot :area :edit nil)= does what the commands do, with a
say in editing. =(screenshot-capture '(x y width height))= takes a screenshot of a
rectangle in layout pixels, =(screenshot-capture nil)= of the current
output. =(select-area function)= runs the area overlay alone and calls
=function= with the chosen rectangle, or =nil= when cancelled, for
tools of your own.
